# Round 2 — online interview

Round 2 is an **online interview of about 60 minutes**, held over video call. There is no take-home
assignment, no code to write, and nothing to submit beforehand. It has two parts: a discussion of
the work you did in round 1, then a general interview.

## Part 1 — your round 1 work (about 45 minutes)

Round 1 was offline and unsupervised, and you were free to use an AI assistant. This part is where
we find out how well you understand what you handed in. We are not looking for new bugs here.

1. **5 minutes** — you summarise how you approached the task: where you started, how you split your
   time.
2. **15 minutes** — you walk us through the bugs you found. We listen and take notes; we will not
   interrupt to grade you.
3. **15 minutes** — we pick a few of your bugs and go deeper: how you reproduced them, why you
   ranked them the way you did, how you know the cause is where you say it is.
4. **5 minutes** — one open question: what did you not get to test, and why would you go there
   first with more time.
5. **5 minutes** — buffer for anything that ran over.

### What we assess

- **Explaining your bugs.** Can you walk a bug back through its steps and name the specific thing
  that makes it wrong, rather than the general symptom.
- **Severity.** Can you rank your own findings and say who is harmed by each one.
- **Depth under questioning.** What happens when we ask why, or ask how you would verify a claim
  you just made.
- **Knowing your own gaps.** "I tested everything" is the weakest answer available. Naming a
  specific area you did not reach, and why it worries you, is the strongest.

## Part 2 — general interview (about 15 minutes)

We talk about your experience: projects you have tested, how you work with developers when a bug
is disputed, and where you want to grow as a QA engineer. The last few minutes are yours, for any
questions you have for us.

## What to bring

- Your round 1 pull request, open and ready to share on screen.
- Access to the site, so you can reproduce a bug live if we ask.

Nothing else. You do not need slides.

## Using an AI assistant

Your use of an AI assistant in round 1 costs you nothing. It only matters here: a report you cannot
stand behind tells us nothing about how you would work with the team.

If some of your round 1 findings came from a tool and you did not verify them yourself, say so at
the start of the session. That is a far better position than being unable to explain them later.
